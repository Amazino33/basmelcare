The shape of the system

BasmelCare is two websites sharing one set of records. What a customer does on one, staff see on the other, immediately.

The staff app
Where the pharmacy is run: the till, stock, money, patient records. Staff sign in with an email address and password given to them by an admin. There is no self sign-up — an account carries a role, a branch, and access to what customers bought, so it is created for you.
The online shop
Where customers browse, order, book a consultation, and look at their own account. They sign in with a code sent to their WhatsApp, or with a password.

Both read and write the same database. A product added by the inventory manager appears in the shop. An order placed at midnight is on the cashier's screen in the morning. A customer's cover, spent at the counter, is gone online too.

Everything is scoped to a branch

Most staff belong to one branch, and see that branch's sales, stock and expenses. Admins have no branch and see everything. If a figure looks wrong or a record has vanished, the branch on the account is the first thing to check.

Roles and what they open

A staff account can hold more than one role, and holding two opens what either one opens — never more. A pharmacist who also covers the till gets both the pharmacist pages and the till; neither role grants the reports page, so the combination does not either.

admin
Everything, across every branch. The only role that can create staff, add branches, change Settings, or read the server logs.
branch_manager
Everything operational within their branch, including prices and the money pages. Cannot create staff or change Settings.
pharmacist
Clinical work. Reviews prescriptions on online orders, decides which products need one, sees medical records, keeps appointments. Reads the catalogue but cannot change a product, a price, or a batch — those are inventory and management decisions, and the guard is on the action, not just the button.
sales
The counter. Builds a sale on the till, prepares online orders, counts stock at stock take.
cashier
Takes the money. Settles invoices the till raised, verifies online payments, records expenses, handles debts and change owed.
inventory_manager
Stock. Products, batches, deliveries, transfers, adjustments, suppliers, purchase orders. Can create a product but not price it — the price saves as zero for a manager to set.
auditor
Reads the money and never touches it. Financial records, reports, expenses, debts, stock received, the cover report. Every write is refused in the action itself, not merely hidden.
promoter
Brings customers in and earns commission on them. Sees customers, appointments and their own commission.
content
Product photographs, and nothing else.

Hidden is not the same as locked

Buttons a role may not use are not rendered, but the refusal lives in the action underneath. A pharmacist cannot save a product even by reaching the action another way, and the auditor cannot record a premium. Both are tested for exactly that.

The counter

Making a sale

Whosalesbranch_manageradmin

Selling is two people's work in two places. The till raises an invoice; the cashier takes the money. Nothing leaves the shelf on the till alone.

  1. Attach the customer, if there is one. Search by name or phone, or create them on the spot. This is what makes wholesale pricing, coupons, debt and monthly cover possible — a walk-in gets none of them.
  2. Search and add products. Type part of the name. If nothing is found, note it as a missed request: it goes on the Can't sell list so somebody can source it.
  3. Set quantities. Each line can be sold loose or by the pack — see below.
  4. Create the invoice. The sale is saved as pending and given a number like INV-20260829-0001. Print it and give it to the customer.
  5. Send them to the cashier. They pay against that number.
  6. Confirm handover once the goods are actually in the customer's hands.

Stock is drawn oldest-expiry-first across batches, so a quantity larger than one batch is spread automatically. You do not pick batches.

Invoice numbers count up and never fill gaps

They run from 0001 each morning. A cancelled sale leaves a gap, and the gap stays — a number is never reissued. That is deliberate: an auditor reading the day's book should find every number between the first and the last, or know a sale is missing.

Taking payment

Whocashierbranch_manageradmin

Open the pending invoice on the Cashier page. One screen settles the whole thing.

Splitting the payment

Cash, card and transfer each have their own box, and a payment can use any combination. If more than one is used the sale is recorded as split, and every naira is still counted under the method it came in on — so the cash, card and transfer filters on the auditor's pages add up to the same totals shown above them.

What happens to the difference

  • They paid too little. With a registered customer the shortfall becomes a debt. A walk-in must pay in full — there is nobody to chase.
  • They paid too much. The excess clears their oldest debts first, oldest to newest. Anything still left is change.
  • Change you cannot give. Tick store as credit and it goes to their wallet instead of the drawer, ready for next time.

Applied before they are asked for anything

In order: a coupon, then their monthly cover, then their stored credit. Whatever remains is the amount to collect.

Online orders

The same page verifies a Paystack payment and approves a pay-on-delivery dispatch.

Prices, packs and wholesale

Who sets pricesadminbranch_manager

Selling and wholesale prices are a commercial decision and belong to management. Everyone else keeps full product and stock management, so a delivery is never blocked — the product simply saves unpriced, at zero, for a manager to set.

Packs

A product can carry a pack size and a pack price. On the till each line can be switched between loose units and packs. The pack price is what you typed, not the unit price multiplied — a pack is usually cheaper than its parts, and the system does not argue with the number you set.

Wholesale

A customer marked wholesale is priced differently, and the price is worked out in this order:

  1. A wholesale price typed onto the product wins outright.
  2. Otherwise it is calculated: the cost of the dearest batch currently in stock, plus a markup percentage.
  3. The markup is a single figure in Settings, and any product may override it.

The dearest batch, not the cheapest and not an average, because that is the one that protects the margin whichever batch the customer walks out with. Wholesale applies at the till and on the shop alike. Buying by the pack at wholesale is the wholesale unit rate multiplied by the pack size.

Debts and change owed

Whocashierbranch_manageradminauditor reads

Two books that mirror each other.

Debt book
What customers owe the pharmacy, from part-paid invoices. Payments can be recorded against a debt at any time, and each one prints its own receipt.
Change owed
What the pharmacy owes customers, from change that was stored rather than handed over. Paying it out prints a receipt too.

Both are visible to the auditor, who can read them and change nothing.

Returns

Whocashiersalesbranch_manageradmin

Returns are taken against the original sale, from Sales History. Open the invoice, choose the quantity coming back on each line, and process it. A return receipt is printed and the stock goes back.

How the money goes back

  • A walk-in is refunded in cash, out of the till. There is no account to credit, so there is nothing to choose.
  • A registered customer can be given store credit, which goes on their account for next time, or cash. Store credit is the default.

The slip says which one happened, and the amount is described the same way on screen — “cash to give back” rather than “credit to be issued” — so nobody hands over money twice or sends a customer away to claim a balance that was never created.

Only cash refunds come off the day's takings

Store credit costs nothing at the moment it is given: the money is still in the drawer, it is simply owed. The takings drop later, when the customer draws it. Profit is a separate question and nets every refund either way, because the sale was undone regardless.

Two rules in Settings govern returns: how many hours after the sale one is still accepted, and whether returns are limited to registered customers. That second rule is off, so walk-ins can return; turn it on only if every return must be tied to a named person.

Calling the pharmacist

Who can callsalescashierbranch_manageradmin

A button in the top bar tells the pharmacist that a customer is waiting. Any pharmacist with the app open sees a banner within about five seconds, and it chimes. They tap On my way and the counter sees that somebody is coming.

If nobody answers on screen within a set wait, their phones can be rung instead — a WhatsApp, falling back to SMS. That is off unless an admin turns it on under Settings → Counter Alerts, because it costs a message through the same gateway that sends receipts.

A call goes quiet on its own after fifteen minutes. Every active pharmacist at that branch with a number on file is messaged once per call; the system holds no rota, so it cannot tell who is actually on duty and tells everyone who could answer.

Medicine

Prescriptions

Who decidespharmacist only

Which products require a prescription is a clinical judgement, so it is the pharmacist's alone. Prescription medicines is the only page in the system no other role can open, including admin.

How it plays out online

  1. A customer puts a listed medicine in their basket. Checkout requires them to upload a prescription before the order can be placed.
  2. The order arrives marked pending review and cannot be prepared until a pharmacist has looked at it.
  3. The pharmacist opens the file, then approves or refuses it. A refusal takes a reason, so the customer can be told something useful.
  4. Only then does the order join the normal queue.

An order that needs no prescription has no review status at all — that is deliberately different from one that needs a review and has not had it yet.

Who may see what

Sales staff can see a prescription attached to an order they are preparing, because they have to. Medical records are pharmacist and admin only and are not on the counter's screens at all. Product photographs are hosted externally for speed; patient documents never are.

The till has no prescription check. Face-to-face, that judgement is the pharmacist's in the room.

Consultations

Whopharmacistsalescashierbranch_manageradminpromoter

Anyone can book a consultation from the shop without an account. Identity comes from the phone number, which is matched to a customer record or creates one — the free allowance is per customer, and an unattached booking could claim it over and over.

Settings control the price per provider type, how many consultations are free and over what period, and how long an unpaid booking holds its slot. Paid consultations go through Paystack.

Bookings appear under Appointments, where staff confirm, reschedule or record the outcome.

Stock

Products and batches

Whoinventory_managerbranch_manageradminpharmacist reads

A product is the thing you sell. A batch is a particular delivery of it, with its own cost, expiry date and quantity. Stock is the sum of the batches; sales draw from the one expiring soonest.

Adding stock

  • Add batch against an existing product — the everyday route when a delivery arrives.
  • Quick add creates the product and its first batch in one form, for something new arriving on the counter.
  • Import takes a spreadsheet. Download the template from the page so the columns match.

Fixing a mistake

Bulk edit was removed. To correct a mistyped delivery, open the product's batches and edit the batch itself — its number, cost and expiry date. Quantity is deliberately not editable there: changing what is on the shelf is a stock adjustment, which is recorded as one and shows up in the movement history.

Names are stored in capitals

However they are typed. Duplicate detection ignores case, so Paracetamol will not sit next to PARACETAMOL as two products.

Photographs

Product images upload on the product form or through the dedicated Product Images page, which is what the content role exists for. Images can be hosted on Cloudinary and resized on the fly; that is switched on in Settings, and existing images are pushed up in batches rather than all at once.

Stock levels and movement

Stock levels
What is on hand, with a printable version for a physical walk-round.
Expiry alerts
What is close to its date. How many days counts as close is a setting.
Transfers
Moving stock between branches, or between storage locations within one.
Adjustments
Correcting what is on the shelf — breakage, loss, a miscount found outside a stock take. Pick the product, then the batch, then say what the true number is and why.
Stock received
What came in and when, which is what an auditor asks for when they want last week's delivery. The auditor can read it.
Can't sell
Things asked for and not stocked, or run out. Buying staff mark an item as sourced once it arrives. Sales staff can see the list — they are the ones who hear the requests — but only buying staff can mark anything.
Movement history
Every change in quantity, with its reason and who made it.

Stock take

Who countssalesbranch_manageradmin

A stock take is counted by one person and approved by another.

  1. Start a count. The system lists what it believes is on the shelf.
  2. Walk the shelves and enter the physical quantity for each line. Filter to what you have not counted yet.
  3. Submit it. Nothing has moved yet — the count is a claim, not a correction.
  4. A manager approves or rejects it. Approving is what writes the differences into stock, each one recorded as a movement.

The person who counted cannot be the person who approves. That is the whole point of the two steps.

Suppliers and purchase orders

Whoinventory_managerbranch_manageradmin

Suppliers hold contact details and what you buy from them. A purchase order lists what was ordered, from whom, and at what cost; receiving it against the delivery is what creates the batches. A purchase order can be cancelled if it never arrives.

Online

The shop

The customer-facing site. Nothing here needs staff attention day to day, but it is worth knowing what customers can do.

Browsing and buying
Products with photographs, a basket, and checkout as a guest or with an account. Guest checkout is deliberate — making people register before they can buy medicine loses the sale.
Signing in
A six-digit code sent to their WhatsApp, or a password. The code works once and is then spent. A phone number works in place of an email address, because that is what people give at the counter.
Their account
Past purchases in store and online, debts, wallet balance, appointments, and their monthly cover if they have any.
Paying
Paystack, or pay on delivery. A Paystack payment is verified by the cashier before the order is prepared.
Delivery or collection
Chosen at checkout. Delivery carries a fee; collection does not.

Online orders

Whosalesbranch_manageradmin
  1. Claim it. One person takes responsibility, and only they can cancel it afterwards.
  2. Check the prescription, if the order carries one. It cannot be prepared until a pharmacist has approved it.
  3. Prepare it. Marking it ready is what takes the stock off the shelf.
  4. Payment. A Paystack payment is verified on the Cashier page; a pay-on-delivery order is approved for dispatch there too.
  5. Dispatch with the name and number of whoever is carrying it, or hand it over for collection.

Cancelling an order that was already marked ready puts the stock back, and gives back any monthly cover it had used.

Wi-Fi on a receipt

Every receipt carries a short code that gives the customer a day of Wi-Fi through the pharmacy's hotspot. How long a day lasts is a setting.

  • The clock starts at the first connection and is never extended. Reconnecting mid-window keeps the same wall-clock expiry.
  • Staff can revoke a receipt's access from Sales History.
  • The code is random and unique to that receipt. An invoice number will not open somebody else's Wi-Fi.
Money

Expenses

Whocashierbranch_manageradminauditor reads

Money spent out of the till — transport, repairs, small purchases. Everyone at a branch sees that branch's whole expense book, not only their own entries, so two cashiers on different shifts are looking at the same page.

A cashier can record an expense and correct one she mistyped. Deleting an expense is management only: the record is the evidence that money left the till, and removing it removes the evidence. Every change to a figure is kept in the money trail.

Financial records

Whoadminbranch_managerauditor reads

What was earned, what it cost, and what actually moved in and out of the till, over any date range.

Trading and cash are reported separately, and never mixed

Buying ₦500,000 of stock and selling half of it is a profitable month on a trading basis and a heavy outflow on a cash basis. Presenting either as "profit" would be wrong, so both are shown and labelled.

The invoice list can be filtered by status and by payment method. A split payment appears under every method that contributed to it, so the filters reconcile with the totals above them rather than quietly excluding splits. Any invoice opens to its full detail.

Reports

Whoadminbranch_managerauditor reads

Revenue, profit, and the three hot-product rankings — most units sold, most revenue, most profit. There is a printable version for the auditor.

Units are counted as packs, not tablets

Counting loose tablets put items like vitamin C at the top of every list simply because they are sold in large numbers. The ranking reflects what actually moves.

The printed top-products sheet shows margin, so it is narrower than the page itself: admin and branch manager only.

Money trail

Whoadminbranch_manager

Every change to a figure that moves money — a selling price, a wholesale price, a coupon's value, a cover plan's premium, a setting — with the old value, the new value, who changed it and when.

It is not a general activity log. It exists to answer one question: who changed this number, and to what.

Cover

Monthly cover

Customers pay a premium each month and draw medicine against it when they need it. Built, tested, and switched off until the pharmacy decides to sell it.

Off by default

Nothing pays for anything at the till or online until an admin turns it on under Settings → Monthly Cover. Until then the till looks exactly as it did and the Cover menu is hidden — but plans can be set up in advance, because they have to exist before it can be turned on.

What a plan is

Who sets plansadminbranch_manager

This is insurance, not a discount card, and the difference is the whole design. A discount costs a known fraction of a sale; cover costs whatever the customer needs, out of stock already paid for. Every setting on a plan exists to keep that bounded.

SettingWhat it doesWhy it is there
Monthly premiumWhat the customer pays each month.The money coming in.
Cover per monthThe most the plan will pay out in a month.Required. Without a ceiling, one subscriber on an expensive course takes a year of everybody's premiums.
Waiting periodDays after the first premium before anything can be claimed. Default 30.Stops somebody joining the morning they fall ill, claiming the lot that afternoon, and never paying again.
Grace periodDays past the paid-to date that cover continues. Default 7.People pay late. Cutting them off at midnight turns a busy week into an uncovered emergency.
Co-payA percentage the subscriber still pays on covered items.Taken off the top, so it stretches the cover rather than draining it — and free medicine is not collected for a cupboard at home.
Not coveredCategories the plan will never pay for.The premium was collected for medicine, not cosmetics.

The plans page shows, for each plan, how many people are on it and what it would cost if every one of them claimed their full allowance this month. That is the figure to look at before agreeing a premium.

A plan is withdrawn rather than deleted, because subscriptions read their cover and waiting period from it and claims already paid under it have to stay explicable.

Signing a customer up

Whocashiersalesbranch_manageradminauditor reads
  1. Find the customer, choose a plan, and take the first premium.
  2. The screen tells you the exact date cover starts. Say it to them. Being told "you are covered" and turned away that afternoon is what loses a customer for good.
  3. Each month, record the premium. Paying early adds a month to the end rather than costing them days.

A customer can hold only one live plan at a time. Cancelling stops cover immediately; premiums already taken are not refunded automatically — do that at the counter.

Using it

Once it is switched on, a subscriber's bill simply drops. At the counter the cashier sees what cover pays and how much is left for the month. If they cannot use it, the screen says why in words that can be read out: the premium is overdue, cover starts on such a date, or this month's cover is used up.

  • Cover is one pool, drawn from at the counter and on the shop alike.
  • Unused cover does not carry into next month. This month's premiums pay this month's claims.
  • Online, the cover is spent when the order is placed, not when it is paid. Cancelling an order gives it back.
  • Delivery is never covered.

Is it working?

Whoadminbranch_managerauditor reads

The cover report gives two figures and refuses to conflate them.

Cash result
Premiums collected, less what the medicine given out actually cost the pharmacy. This is the one that says whether the scheme pays for itself.
Against walking in and buying
Premiums, less what the shop would have taken had those customers simply bought the same medicine. This figure is worse, and it is the one to weigh against the custom the scheme brings in.

Both are broken down per plan, so one that loses money can be repriced rather than the whole scheme abandoned. A table of who is drawing most is there for the same reason an insurer keeps one: a few people claiming many times their premium is normal, and it is only a problem when the pool as a whole stops covering them.

Not built yet

Recurring Paystack billing. A premium can be recorded as paid by Paystack with its reference, but real subscription billing needs a live plan and a webhook, which cannot be exercised until the pharmacy is actually using this.

Growing it

Customers

A customer record holds their name, phone, email, address and notes, and links to everything they have done: purchases, debts, wallet, appointments, medical records, cover.

Customers are marked retail or wholesale, which is what drives wholesale pricing. Who registered a customer is recorded, which is what promoter commission is calculated from.

Coupons

Whoadminbranch_manager

A coupon is a fixed amount or a percentage off, with a cap if you want one. It can be restricted by customer type, order value, item count, category or product, given a usage limit and an expiry date.

A coupon marked auto-apply needs no code — the best applicable one is applied at the till by itself. Coupons require a registered customer.

Messages

Whoadminbranch_manager

One message to many customers — all of them, wholesale only, or those who have bought recently — with an optional picture.

  1. Write the message and choose who it goes to. The page tells you how many people that is.
  2. Prepare it. The recipient list is fixed at that moment, so a broadcast happened to a particular set of people.
  3. Send in batches of twenty. Stopping is safe; nobody is messaged twice, and the rest wait until you come back.
Nobody is put in a group

Each message is sent individually. A group would show every member who else buys medicine here — a fact about named people's health that none of them agreed to share.

Write so the message makes sense without the picture: a customer reached by SMS gets the words only, because a text message cannot carry an image. The page reports how many actually received the picture rather than letting you assume everyone did.

Commissions

Whopromotercashiersalesbranch_manageradmin

Promoters bring customers in and earn a fixed amount per qualifying customer. Each promoter has a code and a monthly target; the amount per customer and the default target are set in Settings.

Promoter codes are handled by the staff app, not the shop — a code sent to the wrong address is refused with an explanation rather than a bare "not found".

Administration

Staff and branches

Whoadmin only

Staff accounts are created here, given one or more roles, and assigned a branch. An account can be deactivated without being deleted — their sales, stock movements and audit entries all point back at it, so nobody can be removed.

The branch matters more than it looks

Anyone who records branch-scoped work — a cashier, a sales person — needs the right branch, or their sales and expenses land somewhere nobody is looking. A cashier created with "all branches" will not appear on any branch's book. Roles that do not record branch work, such as auditor or content, may legitimately have none.

Branches carry a name and a main-branch flag. Storage locations sit inside a branch and are what stock transfers move between.

What catches people out

"The figures do not add up"

Check the branch on the account first. Most records are filtered to the branch of whoever is looking, and a person on the wrong branch sees a book that is genuinely missing rows.

"The customer says they are covered but the till disagrees"

The cover panel says why. It will be one of four things: the first premium was never taken, the waiting period has not ended, the premium is overdue, or this month's cover is already spent. All four are shown in plain words, not a code.

"A wholesale customer is being charged retail"

The customer must be attached to the sale, and marked wholesale on their record. A walk-in gets retail prices whatever they say at the counter.

"An online order will not move"

If it carries a prescription, a pharmacist has to approve it first. Nobody else can, including admin.

"A product will not save with a price"

Only admin and branch manager can set prices. Everyone else can create the product — it saves at zero for a manager to price, so a delivery is never held up.

"The stock take did not change anything"

Submitting is a claim, not a correction. A manager still has to approve it, and approval is what writes the differences into stock.

"A customer cannot use their Wi-Fi code"

The window runs from the first connection, not from the purchase, and cannot be extended by reconnecting. Check whether it was revoked from Sales History.
